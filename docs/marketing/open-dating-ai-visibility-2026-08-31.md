# OpenDating AI and Search Visibility Audit

**Snapshot date:** 2026-08-31

**Product:** OpenDating

**Official URL supplied for this audit:** [opendating-mobile.expo.app](https://opendating-mobile.expo.app/)

**Category:** Privacy-focused dating app

**Audience:** Privacy-conscious singles

**Core job:** Find compatible dates without exposing a phone number, email address, or precise location, and without an addictive swipe feed

**Named competitors:** Tinder, Bumble, Hinge

## Method and definitions

The five phrases below were submitted verbatim in two lanes: an unquoted web search and a fresh, isolated ChatGPT web-search conversation for each query. The first four did not contain the OpenDating name. This is a dated sample of one search engine and one assistant, **not** a claim that every assistant or user will see the same results. Results vary by engine, model, locale, personalization, and time.

A supplementary branded search (`OpenDating privacy dating app`) and direct checks of the supplied official origin were used only to diagnose gaps; they are not included in the five-query mention totals.

- **Mentioned** means the returned result text or linked source named OpenDating as a product. The product name appearing in the query itself does not count.
- **Cited with a live link** means a returned result linked to an OpenDating-owned product, documentation, repository, or another source specifically about OpenDating. A bare name is not a citation.
- Ranks below are the order in this snapshot, not an endorsement of product quality or claim accuracy.

## What the sources reward

The results repeatedly favored pages with:

1. An exact-match title and a direct answer near the top.
2. A visible methodology or comparison criteria.
3. Concrete privacy mechanics, price, audience, and tradeoffs.
4. A recent publication or review date.
5. Links to official product or policy pages.
6. A recognized entity with independent coverage.

The competitor context is supportable from first-party sources:

- [Bumble says Incognito Mode](https://bumble.com/features/incognito-mode/) hides a profile unless the member likes someone first; it is a Premium or Premium+ feature.
- [Hinge says members can set a broader location manually](https://help.hinge.co/hc/en-us/articles/49661615922195-Location-Settings), displays the selected neighborhood rather than an exact address, and does not automatically update location.
- [Tinder's own safety guidance](https://policies.tinder.com/web/safety-center/guide/basics/in/en/) advises members not to share location or a phone number until comfortable and to take time before moving conversations off-app.
- [OpenDating's official repository](https://github.com/jongan69/opendating-mobile#what-is-opendating) describes one contextual introduction instead of an endless feed, a five-character geohash of roughly 5 km, private decisions, and end-to-end encrypted direct messages. Those claims did not surface in any audited query.

## Visibility assessment

**Visibility score: Weak overall; effectively invisible for unbranded discovery.**

OpenDating received zero mentions in the web-search lane and zero mentions across the four unbranded ChatGPT prompts. Only the branded ChatGPT comparison produced a mention and citation, and that citation led to the protocol repository rather than the live web app. The branded web search still substituted Hinge and returned no OpenDating source.

The main gaps are:

- **No generic discovery:** zero share of voice across privacy, alternatives, safety, and price intent.
- **Limited and misclassified branded recognition:** ChatGPT found the protocol repository, but search did not; neither lane cited the live web app.
- **No independent authority:** the supplementary branded search surfaced the owner's GitHub profile and package ecosystem, but not meaningful third-party product coverage.
- **Name ambiguity:** "OpenDating" is a generic phrase and overlaps with unrelated uses such as open dating, `#open`, and Opendate.
- **Conflicting canonical signals on the live deployment:** the Expo homepage canonicals to `opendating-mobile.expo.app`, while its live [`robots.txt`](https://opendating-mobile.expo.app/robots.txt), [`sitemap.xml`](https://opendating-mobile.expo.app/sitemap.xml), [`llms.txt`](https://opendating-mobile.expo.app/llms.txt), blog pages, and GitHub About link point to `jongan69.github.io/opendating-mobile`. This splits the entity and crawl authority across origins.
- **Missing comparison target:** the live [`/blog/tinder-vs-bumble-vs-opendating/`](https://opendating-mobile.expo.app/blog/tinder-vs-bumble-vs-opendating/) URL is a valid `noindex` correction notice for a withdrawn article, so there is no indexable, source-reviewed page answering the exact branded comparison query.
- **Recommendation readiness:** the official materials truthfully call the product pre-release and describe remaining production blockers. Assistants should not be expected to recommend it as broadly available until availability and release evidence support that claim.

## Recommended actions

### 1. Consolidate the product entity and canonical origin

Choose one public canonical origin and align all of the following to it: page canonicals, `robots.txt` sitemap URL, every sitemap `<loc>`, `llms.txt` links, JSON-LD `url` and `sameAs`, GitHub About, repository README, and social profiles. Redirect alternate origins to the canonical one where the hosting stack permits it. This is the highest-priority technical fix because current signals tell crawlers that the same product lives at different origins.

### 2. Publish three indexable, source-reviewed answer pages

Create the minimum pages that map to demonstrated intent:

- **Privacy category:** “Privacy-focused dating app for people who do not want precise location tracking.”
- **Problem guide:** “How to date online without sharing your phone number or precise location.”
- **Comparison:** “Tinder vs Bumble vs OpenDating,” with a visible last-reviewed date, neutral criteria, limitations, and links to official competitor policy/help pages.

Do not revive unsupported claims from the withdrawn comparison. A new comparison should distinguish what the app sends to its own service, what matches can see, what is encrypted, what is still pre-release, and what mainstream competitors actually document.

### 3. Make product facts easy to cite

On the canonical product page, publish a short fact block and `SoftwareApplication` structured data covering category, audience, platforms, current availability, truthful price, location handling, messaging encryption boundary, account requirements, and release status. Give each material claim a stable first-party URL. Keep `llms.txt` as a supplement; it does not replace crawlable HTML or external authority.

### 4. Earn independent corroboration

Seek technically competent reviews or coverage that can verify the coarse-location implementation, encrypted-message boundary, business model, source availability, and limitations. Publish a threat model, privacy architecture, and reproducible test evidence, then pitch those primary materials to privacy, open-source, dating-safety, and decentralized-technology publications. AI citations are materially more likely when a claim is corroborated outside the product's own site.

### 5. Build an explicit alternatives and pricing footprint

Once the product is genuinely available, maintain an accurate pricing/availability page and a source-backed “Tinder alternatives for privacy-conscious singles” page. The “for startups” query is mismatched to the ICP, so optimize for singles and privacy intent rather than producing founder-oriented content merely to match that phrase.

### 6. Submit, inspect, and repeat the audit

Submit the canonical sitemap to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, inspect the three answer URLs, and request recrawls after canonical consolidation. Repeat these exact five queries monthly across web search and named AI assistants. Store the verbatim answer, links, model, date, locale, product mention, product citation, and rank separately; search visibility and AI recommendation behavior are related but not interchangeable.

## Bottom line

OpenDating has a differentiated privacy story, but it is absent from generic assistant recommendations. A branded prompt can recover the protocol repository, which is better than total invisibility, but the live product is not yet the entity being cited. The immediate work is entity consolidation plus a small set of indexable, source-backed pages answering the exact intents already producing competitor citations.

## Remediation prepared from this audit

The August 31 release change set addresses every repository-controlled gap:

- `opendating-mobile.expo.app` is the single canonical origin in the app shell, sitemap, robots file, generated articles, product facts, repository metadata, and machine-readable product summary.
- The app shell and crawlable product-facts page publish `SoftwareApplication` structured data with the live URL, free web availability, and official repository relationships.
- Four fail-closed, source-reviewed pages now answer privacy-category, no-phone-account, coarse-location, and exact Tinder/Bumble/OpenDating comparison intent.
- The comparison distinguishes mention from citation, links current first-party competitor sources, and states OpenDating's early-network, recovery, operator, and native-distribution limits.
- Stale generated articles are removed on every build, while unreviewed Markdown remains excluded unless it explicitly declares `draft: false`.

Search-engine recrawling, Search Console/Bing submission, independent reviews, and future assistant citations remain external propagation and authority lanes. They cannot be created truthfully by changing first-party source alone and should be measured again after indexing.
