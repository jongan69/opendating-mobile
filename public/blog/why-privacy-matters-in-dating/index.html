Why Privacy Matters in Dating Apps

August 2026

Dating apps know extraordinary amounts about you. Your location — often updated every few minutes. Your photos, bio, age, gender, and sexual orientation. Every profile you've viewed. Every person you've liked, passed on, or matched with. Every message you've ever sent. When you're active, where you go, what times you use the app.

This isn't metadata. It's a near-complete profile of your romantic and social life, stored on a company's servers.

What Dating Apps Collect

Most major dating apps collect:

  • Precise GPS coordinates — updated frequently, often every time you open the app
  • All messages — stored in plaintext on their servers, accessible to employees
  • Behavioral data — every swipe, like, pass, match, and conversation
  • Device identifiers — linking your dating activity to other apps and websites
  • Purchase history — subscriptions, boosts, super likes
  • Photos — stored permanently, even after you delete them from your profile

This data is used to train recommendation algorithms and, in many cases, sold to data brokers or used for targeted advertising. Your dating preferences become an ad-targeting signal.

The Stakes Are Higher Than You Think

Unlike your shopping habits or music taste, dating data is deeply personal. A data breach at a dating app doesn't expose credit card numbers — it exposes your sexual orientation, your relationship history, your private conversations, and your real-time location patterns.

This has real consequences. In countries where homosexuality is criminalized, a data breach at a dating app could put users at risk of prosecution, violence, or death. Even in countries with strong privacy laws, dating data has been used in divorce proceedings, employment decisions, and blackmail.

How OpenDating Is Different

Privacy Isn't Just About Hiding

Privacy in dating isn't about secrecy — it's about control. It's about deciding who gets to know what about you, and when. It's about being able to explore connections without creating a permanent, searchable record. It's about safety.

When a dating app knows your exact location, your message history, and your behavior patterns, you're not the customer — you're the product. OpenDating flips that model. You own your data. You control your visibility. You decide what to share.
