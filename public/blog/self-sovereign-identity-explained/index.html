Self-Sovereign Identity: What It Means for Dating

August 2026

OpenDating identifies an account with a cryptographic keypair rather than an email-and-password login. This gives the member control of the signing identity, but it does not make the first-party service unable to moderate an account or remove hosted profile data.

The keypair can work in a compatible client. Full profile, match, message-history, and private-list portability across providers is post-GA work and should not be inferred from key portability alone.

How It Works

In OpenDating, your account is a Nostr keypair — a public key (your identity) and a private key (your secret, stored only on your device). When you create a profile, it's cryptographically signed by your private key. Any OpenDating-compatible client can verify that signature and know it's really you.

This means:

  • Client portability — a compatible client can use the same signing identity
  • Service limits — hosted profile and relationship data still depend on the current operator
  • Prove it's you — your public key is your identity; anyone can verify messages come from you
  • Privacy-focused — your private key is stored with device secure storage and leaves only when you explicitly use the legacy recovery export

Why This Matters for Dating

Dating is personal. The idea that a corporation owns your dating profile — your photos, your bio, your preferences, your conversation history — is strange when you think about it. Your dating life shouldn't be an asset on a company's balance sheet.

If OpenDating disappears, the keypair still exists, but the current service data may not. Signed export, recovery, and provider-migration formats must be implemented and tested before broader portability claims are made.

What About Lost Keys?

This is the trade-off. With a centralized app, you can reset your password. With self-sovereign identity, losing your private key means losing access to your account — permanently. There's no "forgot password" button. This is why OpenDating provides backup and export features, and why you should store your key somewhere safe.

The current v0.1 app stores the key with device secure storage and loads it into a JavaScript signer. A native device-bound signer and passkey-encrypted recovery are required before production; the beta does not claim that compromise is impossible.

The Bigger Picture

Self-sovereign identity isn't just for dating. It's part of a broader movement toward user-owned digital identity — where you control your online presence across platforms without being locked into any single provider. OpenDating is one of the first consumer applications to put this into practice in a way that feels natural and polished.
