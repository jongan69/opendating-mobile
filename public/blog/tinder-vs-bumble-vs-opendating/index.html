Location: The Biggest Difference

All three major apps request precise GPS access and update your location frequently — often every time you open the app. This data can reveal where you live, where you work, and your daily routines. OpenDating converts your GPS to a coarse geohash (~5 km precision) entirely on-device and only sends that prefix. The raw coordinates are discarded.

Messages: Who Can Read Them

On Tinder, Bumble, and Hinge, messages are stored on company servers and are accessible to employees (subject to internal access controls). Reports of inappropriate behavior include the full conversation history being reviewed by human moderators at the company. OpenDating uses NIP-44 end-to-end encryption — messages are encrypted on your device and can only be decrypted by the recipient. Not even the relay operator can read them.

Business Model Implications

Tinder, Bumble, and Hinge are all owned by Match Group, a publicly traded company. Their business model depends on collecting user data for ad targeting, algorithmic recommendations, and upsells (Super Likes, Boosts, premium subscriptions). OpenDating has no advertising, no data monetization, and no premium tiers. It's free and open source.

Which App Should You Use?

If you value a large user base and don't mind trading privacy for convenience, the major apps serve that purpose. If you want a dating experience where you control your data, your location stays private, and your messages are genuinely encrypted, OpenDating offers a fundamentally different architecture.
